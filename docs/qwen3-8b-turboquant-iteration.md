# Qwen3-8B TurboQuant local iteration

Recorded: 2026-11-11. These are operator-reported local WSL and Docker results, not a reproducible benchmark or a validated SEC-answer evaluation. No application launcher or production configuration changed.

## Test setup

- GPU: RTX 5060 Laptop GPU, 8,151 MiB reported VRAM.
- Model: `/home/redsandr/models/Qwen3-8B-Q4_K_M.gguf`.
- Candidate: `TheTom/llama-cpp-turboquant`, branch `feature/turboquant-kv-cache`, commit `643956891dfdf51aecdbd05ca508dd21e21e8195`. Image: `rve-turboquant-thetom:6439568`, built from `.devops/cuda.Dockerfile` with `CUDA_DOCKER_ARCH=120`.
- Both tested cache configurations used 4,096 context, batch size 32, parallelism 1, and the same model weights. The server bound its Docker host port to `127.0.0.1:8083`. The 30-layer runs used `--n-gpu-layers 30`.
- Control: `--cache-type-k f16 --cache-type-v f16`. Candidate: `--cache-type-k q8_0 --cache-type-v turbo3`. The server accepted these flags. `docker inspect` confirmed the candidate container's command arguments. A later startup with `-lv 4` printed the actual candidate KV-cache allocation: 209.25 MiB across 4,096 cells and 36 layers, with K (`q8_0`) at 153.00 MiB and V (`turbo3`) at 56.25 MiB. That verifies the candidate's cache types for this run. The earlier `f16/f16` control did not have an equivalent captured cache-allocation log.

### Chipotle filing excerpt: contradiction on the candidate

The operator sent a manually supplied excerpt from [Chipotle's Q2 2024 Form 10-Q, Labor Costs](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1058090/000105809024000032/cmg-20240630.htm), not a passage retrieved by the application. For the three months ended June 30, labor costs rose from $611.7 million in 2023 to $716.6 million in 2024, while their share of revenue fell from 24.3% to 24.1%. The filing attributes the percentage change in part to sales leverage, partly offset by restaurant wage inflation. A prompt asked the model to separate the dollar amount from the revenue share, name the periods and explanation, and quote only words in the excerpt. Both configurations used the same excerpt, prompt, model weights, and server settings, with temperature 0 and `max_tokens=384`.

The `q8_0/turbo3` candidate correctly reported both numeric directions, then contradicted itself: "The excerpt does not establish whether labor costs increased or decreased in absolute terms, but it does show the dollar amount increased." The same contradiction appeared again after the candidate server restarted. The second request reported `cached_tokens=0`; its response ended normally after 152 generated tokens. The `f16/f16` control did not make that contradiction in its two reported responses. Both configurations used an ellipsis inside a quoted passage instead of reproducing one contiguous exact quote. The control also described a fall from 24.3% to 24.1% as "0.2%" rather than the clearer "0.2 percentage points." Repeated deterministic output on one prompt is a reproducible local observation, not proof that KV quantization caused the difference.

The operator then tested three additional cache pairs against the same 243-token Chipotle prompt, one server at a time, using the same image, model weights, 30 GPU layers, 4,096 context, temperature 0, and `max_tokens=384`. Each reported `cached_tokens=0` and `finish_reason: stop`:

| K / V | Result on the Chipotle excerpt |
| --- | --- |
| `q8_0 / f16` | Correct dollar and revenue-share directions, period, and management explanation. No contradiction. The quoted fragment from the supplied excerpt was contiguous. |
| `f16 / turbo3` | Correct dollar and revenue-share directions, but added an awkward claim that the excerpt does not specify the "exact dollar amount of the decrease in labor costs as a share of revenue." Its quote used an ellipsis. This did not reproduce the `q8_0/turbo3` contradiction. |
| `q8_0 / turbo4` | Correct dollar and revenue-share directions, period, and management explanation. No contradiction. The reported answer matched the earlier `q8_0/f16` answer on this prompt. |

This small ablation does not isolate V `turbo3` as the root cause. The specific contradiction appeared only for the tested `q8_0/turbo3` pair, while `f16/turbo3` showed a different wording problem. The `q8_0/turbo4` run did not include a captured internal KV-cache allocation log or a broader quality test.

### Chipotle food-cost excerpt: period and missing-evidence checks

A second manually supplied excerpt from the same [SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1058090/000105809024000032/cmg-20240630.htm), under Food, Beverage and Packaging Costs, gave three-month costs of $738.7 million in 2023 and $873.7 million in 2024, both at 29.4% of revenue. It also gave six-month costs of $1,431.2 million and $1,652.7 million, with revenue shares of 29.3% and 29.1%, respectively. The excerpt included management's explanation for the **three-month share only**. Both `f16/f16` and `q8_0/turbo4` correctly said the three-month dollar amount rose while the share stayed flat, and the six-month share fell. Both answers ended normally on a 342-token prompt with `cached_tokens=0`.

The next question asked for the **six-month** share change and its reason **based only on that excerpt**, without transferring the three-month explanation. `q8_0/turbo4` reported the fall from 29.3% to 29.1% and said the excerpt gave no specific six-month reason; it ended normally on a 354-token prompt with `cached_tokens=271`. The `f16/f16` control reported the decline but attributed it to "improved cost management and pricing strategies," which the supplied excerpt did not state. It ended normally with `cached_tokens=0`. This is an unsupported claim **relative to the supplied excerpt**, not a claim that the complete SEC filing lacks a six-month explanation; the full filing contains a separate six-month discussion. The two runs had different prompt-cache states and are not a speed comparison. Neither test exercised application retrieval.

The [fork's KV-cache guide](https://github.com/TheTom/llama-cpp-turboquant/blob/643956891dfdf51aecdbd05ca508dd21e21e8195/docs/KV-cache-quantization.md) warns that fluent text is not enough to establish quality and recommends a logit or KL-divergence comparison. Its [quality benchmark note](https://github.com/TheTom/llama-cpp-turboquant/blob/643956891dfdf51aecdbd05ca508dd21e21e8195/docs/quality-benchmarks.md) records a historical severe `turbo3` perplexity failure involving MoE hybrid memory, not this dense Qwen3-8B test. [PR #281](https://github.com/TheTom/llama-cpp-turboquant/pull/281) reports CUDA Qwen3-8B-Q8_0 tests on another toolchain; it closed without a merge and does not validate this Q4_K_M build. [Issue #241](https://github.com/TheTom/llama-cpp-turboquant/issues/241) documents a fixed `turbo3` corruption bug on AMD Polaris Vulkan, not NVIDIA CUDA. None establishes the cause of the local contradiction.

At 30 GPU layers, operator-reported idle VRAM use was 5,855 MiB for the candidate and 6,147 MiB for the control. This is a **292 MiB difference in total reported GPU memory use**, not an isolated KV-cache saving. Other processes, allocator behavior, and server state were not controlled.

The 2,850-token retrieval request took 64.6 seconds to process the prompt and generated at 16.04 tokens per second with the candidate (`cache_n=3`). The control took 30.8 seconds and generated at 18.21 tokens per second (`cache_n=0`). These are single runs. They do not establish a speed regression or speedup. A short 30-layer candidate request reported 22.56 tokens per second; short requests at 10 and 20 layers used different response lengths or cache states and are not a matched benchmark.

## Decision and next iteration

The `q8_0/turbo3` candidate completed several short and roughly 2,800-token requests with correct answers, but also produced the repeated contradiction on a filing excerpt. The `f16/f16` control made an unsupported attribution on a different, deliberately limited excerpt. `q8_0/turbo4` answered these excerpt checks correctly, making it a candidate for more testing rather than an approved replacement. Do not use any cache setting as an SEC-answer backend without a reliable contradiction and evidence gate. These results do not establish the root cause, general quality parity, production readiness, or faster inference. The official CUDA image remains the known working 8B path. The existing 7B launcher stays unchanged.

1. Keep the candidate's `-lv 4` cache-allocation result as the direct type check. Capture the corresponding `f16/f16` allocation under the same context and layer settings before attributing memory differences solely to KV-cache compression.
2. Extend the fixed prompt set to other reviewed filing excerpts. Compare `f16/f16`, `q8_0/turbo3`, and `q8_0/turbo4` one server at a time. Treat any contradiction as a failed answer. Record the response, cache setting, `finish_reason`, and prompt-cache count. Confirm internal `turbo4` allocation separately. Run the real SEC retrieval and evidence workflow separately; a manually supplied excerpt does not test retrieval.
3. Benchmark one server at a time with the same 30-layer setup, prompt, context, generation limit, cache state, and repeated trials. Record prompt and decode timings separately, along with GPU memory. Stop if answers degrade or the server fails.
4. Test larger contexts only after quality checks pass and memory headroom is confirmed under load. Do not transplant the 35B MoE benchmark's flags or 256K context to this 8B model.

Earlier fork outcomes remain separate: `unixsysdev/llama-turboquant` with `tq3_0` produced repeated words despite loading; `atomicmilkshake/llama-cpp-turboquant` allocated `turbo4/turbo3` but failed during warm-up with a missing `turbo_innerq_needs_tensor_update` symbol. Neither is evidence that TheTom fails or that all TurboQuant variants work.

### NLI follow-up

A subsequent operator run produced one supported claim and two `neutral` claims, rather than the earlier three unsupported claims. The two rejected claims had maximum entailment scores of 0.4525 and 0.4451, both above the configured 0.40 threshold. Replaying those exact claims against the retrieved passages on CPU showed that chunk 6 predicted `entailment` at those scores, while chunk 5 predicted `neutral` with higher winning-label scores of 0.5537 and 0.4750. `NLIClient.verify()` had picked the highest winning-label score across all passages, so a non-entailing passage displaced an entailing one. The code now keeps an above-threshold entailing passage unless a contradiction prediction has at least as high a winning-label score. Unit tests cover both cases; the reported CUDA run has not been repeated after the change. The answer's wording still says "0.2%" instead of the more precise "0.2 percentage points".

The next operator run showed that the first claim still failed. Chunk 6 predicted `entailment` at 0.4202, but chunk 5 predicted `contradiction` at 0.6735. Since the structured claim cites chunk 6 and its quotation has passed validation, NLI now checks each structured claim against its own cited passage instead of the first three retrieved passages. A replay of the two exact operator claims against chunk 6 on CPU returned `entailment` scores of 0.4202 and 0.4495; both pass at the 0.40 threshold. This does not replace a post-change CUDA end-to-end test or validate arithmetic by itself.
